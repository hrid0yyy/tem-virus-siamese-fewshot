\textit{Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine}

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the original research article \textbf{``Siamese metric learning for generalised few-shot recognition of rare viruses in transmission electron microscopy images''} for consideration in \textit{Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine}.

\textbf{Why this journal.} The public TEM virus dataset and its benchmark were introduced in this journal by Matuszewski and Sintorn (2021). This work is a direct methodological follow-up on that dataset and its official split. It develops and evaluates a computing method for a diagnostic imaging task, and releases the software, protocol and per-patch predictions for reuse. This matches the journal's focus on computing methods and software for biomedical research and practice.

\textbf{What is known.} The benchmark covers the 14 most frequent viruses. Subsequent studies have reported higher 14-class accuracy, but several used protocols other than the official micrograph-level split. The two studies that used all 22 classes trained a closed-set classifier on every class. As far as I could determine, neither reported rare-class results on the official split or treated the rare viruses as few-shot classes. Yet rare viruses, with only 5--25 training micrographs each, are the realistic diagnostic case: an emerging virus will always have few reference images.

\textbf{What this study adds.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{A first protocol for the rare viruses.} To my knowledge, this is the first generalised few-shot evaluation of all 22 classes on the official split. The eight rare viruses are never used for training, every hyper-parameter is chosen on validation, and a pixel-level audit confirms there is no train/test leakage.
\item \textbf{A fair comparison on the benchmark.} A Siamese supervised-contrastive encoder matches a faithful re-implementation of the benchmark classifier on the 14 classes: \SiamClosedAcc\,\% versus \BOneClosedAcc\,\% accuracy over three seeds each, with no significant seed-pair difference. Both exceed the published 93.1\,\%.
\item \textbf{A one-shot advantage.} With a single example per rare virus, the Siamese features recognise rare viruses better than cross-entropy features (\KshotSiamOne\,\% vs \KshotBOneOne\,\% rare-class accuracy). The difference is significant in all seed pairs.
\item \textbf{A diagnosis and a simple fix for the rare-class failures.} The confused rare classes, including Dengue versus Sapovirus, are nearly linearly separable in the learned features. A validation-selected logistic regression on frozen features therefore raises 22-class macro-F1 from \SiamNcmFOne\ to \SiamLrFOne, significantly on every checkpoint, without retraining.
\item \textbf{Transparent reporting.} Negative and neutral results are reported in full, including where the Siamese approach gives no advantage. Statistics use micrograph-level cluster bootstrap and paired tests.
\end{itemize}
