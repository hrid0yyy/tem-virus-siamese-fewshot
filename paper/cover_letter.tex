Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the original research article \textbf{``Identifying rare viruses in negative-stain transmission electron microscopy images from few examples with Siamese neural networks''} for consideration in \textit{Array}. The manuscript was previously submitted to \textit{Micron}, whose editors suggested \textit{Array} as a more suitable journal.

\textbf{Why this journal.} The study addresses a core machine-learning problem, generalised few-shot recognition, in which new classes must be recognised from a handful of examples alongside the classes seen in training. It applies metric learning to a real computer-vision task, the identification of viruses in negative-stain transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images, where rare and newly emerging viruses have very few reference images. The emphasis on a fair comparison with a strong baseline, validation-only model selection, leakage control and fully released code fits the scope of \textit{Array} in machine learning, computer vision and medical informatics.

\textbf{What is known.} The public TEM virus dataset of Matuszewski and Sintorn (2021) contains negative-stain micrographs of 22 viruses, but its benchmark covers only the 14 most frequent. The eight rare viruses, with 5--25 training micrographs each, have been either excluded or pooled into closed-set classifiers trained on every class; as far as I could determine, no study has evaluated them as viruses recognised from a few examples on the official split.

\textbf{What this study adds.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Recognition of rare viruses from few examples.} A Siamese network trained on the 14 frequent viruses is applied to the eight rare viruses, which it never saw in training and which are added from their reference images alone. With a single reference particle per rare virus, its features recognise rare viruses better than features of a conventionally trained classifier (\KshotSiamOne\,\% versus \KshotBOneOne\,\% rare-class accuracy), significantly in all seed pairs.
\item \textbf{A fair comparison.} On the 14 frequent viruses, the network matches a faithful re-implementation of the benchmark classifier (\SiamClosedAcc\,\% versus \BOneClosedAcc\,\% accuracy, three seeds each), under identical, leakage-audited evaluation.
\item \textbf{Which viruses are confused, and why.} Errors occur mainly between viruses that look alike in negative-stain TEM: among the arenaviruses (Guanarito, LCM, Machupo and Lassa virus), between the parapoxviruses Pseudocowpox and Orf, and between small, near-spherical particles such as Dengue virus and Sapovirus. These pairs indicate where expert confirmation and additional reference images are most needed.
\item \textbf{A simple remedy.} The confused viruses are nearly linearly separable in the learned features, and a linear classifier on the frozen features raises the 22-class macro-F1 from \SiamNcmFOne\ to \SiamLrFOne\ without retraining.
\item \textbf{Open and transparent.} Negative results are reported in full, and the code, trained models and per-patch predictions are publicly available.
\end{itemize}
